## Introduction

Sites that check age today use a button, send people to a third-party service, or ask for an ID. The [^agecheck^] element gives the site a form control instead. The browser asks the person before it shares anything, and the site gets an age group or an error.

The answer is declared, not verified. Parents can already set a child's age on the platform, in the parental controls on the child's device. A website has no way to ask for that age. To the server, the answer is an honor system, like today's "I'm over 18" button; see [[[#people-left-out-and-old-buttons]]] and [[[#proof-for-regulators]]].
